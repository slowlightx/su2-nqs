## 9. Core architectures and exact symmetry guarantees

### Ordinary Spatial-ViT and explicit projection

Spatial patches/coordinates and spin configurations enter a full neural network, which outputs complex amplitudes or log amplitudes. Fixed $S^z=0$, spin inversion, and coherent amplitude projection onto the full spatial $\Gamma-A_1$ sector constrain different symmetries; they do not guarantee $S=0$. A strict singlet requires actually applying the complete spin projector $P_{S=0}\psi_\theta$ and using that same state definition in every update, sampling operation, and H action. Variation after projection (VAP) retains the network's variational freedom, but fully projected amplitudes, gradients, and H action are expensive; approximate or subset projection must not be called exact SU(2).

### ClebschTree / finite Schur decoder

Each internal **edge** carries the representation space

\[
\mathcal V_e=\bigoplus_q\bigl(\mathbb C^{d_{e,q}}\otimes V_{q/2}\bigr),\qquad
D_e=\sum_q d_{e,q}(q+1).
\]

Here $q=2j$; $d_{e,q}$ is the retained multiplicity, whereas $D_e$ is the total virtual dimension including magnetic degeneracy. The learnable reduced tensor $W$ at each node connects the legal spin and multiplicity channels of its input/output edges. Fixed CG coefficients/intertwiners determine the magnetic-index dependence, and the root is fixed to $q=0$. Changing $W$ preserves the global singlet as long as these structures remain intact. The so-called "combination weights on edges" are induced by contracting the node tensors $W$, rather than constituting another set of variables that must be learned independently.

The free-tensor model directly optimizes the legal $W$ blocks. The prescribed $q$ and $d_{e,q}$ define the capacity truncation: training can change linear combinations within the retained space, but does not automatically restore discarded channels. A singlet root does not imply that every edge must mechanically become wider toward the root; capacity must be allocated according to the entanglement across physical cuts and in complete multiplets. Tree cuts, finite multiplicity width, sign structure, and optimization conditioning can all limit accuracy.

**Selecting multiplicities using entanglement.** At fixed $q$, $W_q$ compresses legal products of child blocks into $d_{e,q}$ directions. It therefore defines a low-rank representation, but the singular values of a local $W_q$ are not physical Schmidt weights. A canonicalized tree can satisfy $W_q^\dagger W_q=I$, with all singular values equal to 1; importance is determined by the complementary environment of the entire state. In orthonormal bases across a cut, take the SVD of the normalized singlet coefficient matrix $C_q$, $C_q=U_qs_qV_q^\dagger$. The weights $s_{q,\alpha}^2$ belong to the multiplicity density $\sigma_q$; the physical region RDM has eigenvalues $s_{q,\alpha}^2/(q+1)$ with degeneracy $q+1$. Retaining one complete multiplet retains total probability $s_{q,\alpha}^2$ and consumes physical bond dimension $q+1$; the retained probability must not be multiplied by $q+1$ again. Density-matrix truncation within each $q$ gives optimal fidelity compression of a specified complete state, not necessarily optimal energy. Transfer must additionally include the new boundary's $H$ response: directions weakly occupied in the old state may carry the new screening processes. A raw single-tree environment is not automatically the environment of the full coherent $A_1$ superposition; the latter still requires independent validation.

A three-spin block decomposes as

\[
\tfrac12\otimes\tfrac12\otimes\tfrac12
=(\mathbb C^2\otimes V_{1/2})\oplus V_{3/2}.
\]

Both copies of the $j=1/2$ channel are retained; the joint three-input core jointly parameterizes all legal intermediate channels. A binary basis used to evaluate CG coefficients is a representation-theoretic tool, not a factorization of the learnable core into two restricted sequential two-input layers. $C_3$ equivariance additionally requires the correct action of cyclic permutations on multiplicity channels; it does not follow automatically from having "three inputs." Both binary and joint-ternary structures remain under consideration, with no assumed winner.

Global lattice translations, rotations, and reflections require an actual coherent projection or a corresponding equivariant implementation; the tree's geometric layout alone does not guarantee full A1. Exact symmetry guarantees must be reported for the implementation of each configuration/state, rather than transferred from one approach to another.

### How attention can be integrated

The Vieijra–Nys Fig.3 scan at 8×8, J2=.5 supports increasing \(\tau\) first; their production choice is \(\tau_{j\le2}=16,\tau_{j>2}=8,j_{\max}=4\). After increasing \(\tau\) and at other couplings, higher \(j\) still matters. ### Effective boundary environments: constructing larger initial states from small-system subspaces (2026-10-23)

Small clusters must pass upward not only low-energy states, but also information about "what boundary exchange can turn them into." Decompose $H=H_A+H_B+V_\partial$, $V_\partial=\sum_{ij}J_{ij}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j$; explicitly specify all actual J1/J2 bonds on the new boundary, periodic seams, and site ordering. Three levels of "environment" must be distinguished rather than collectively described as completed:

1. **Geometric bath / correlated product bath (implemented).** From the Gram matrix of exterior response vectors, $G_{jk}=\langle w_j|w_k\rangle$, construct $K=JG^T J^T$ and contract low-rank factors with the interior response $R_{i,a,s}$ without forming a large multiplicity covariance matrix. $G=I$ is only an independent-bath approximation. More precisely, for a region separated by exactly one edge of a **raw single tree**, the Schmidt rank is bounded by $D_e=\sum_q(q+1)d_{e,q}$, hence $S_A\leq\log D_e$. If the true entropy across that cut grows with its perimeter, increasing tree depth alone cannot remove this restriction. Full coherent A1 is a coherent sum of many permuted tree states, and a fixed physical region cuts different tree edges in different frames. The single-tree $D_e$ bound therefore cannot be applied directly to the projected state. Projection can increase expressive power, but also increases query and optimization-environment costs. ## 12. Article and 15-minute presentation framework

### 12.1 Current article positioning: version after the discussion with Eunah (2026-10-29 EDT)

**Working title: Symmetry-controlled neural spectroscopy of frustrated magnets beyond exact diagonalization.**

These are the **article goal and intended introduction framing** agreed upon in today's discussion with Eunah, not an abstract of completed results. **Draft framing (English):**

Low-energy states of frustrated magnets can be close in variational energy while displaying different magnetic, singlet, and excitation structures. We pursue a symmetry-controlled neural description of the spin-1/2 triangular-lattice J1-J2 Heisenberg model, combining competitive variational accuracy with exact total-spin and lattice-quantum-number control. The purpose is not to infer that unconstrained neural states are physically incorrect, but to remove spin-sector ambiguity in the quantities used to identify low-energy organization.

Exact SU(2) symmetry fixes the total-spin identity of each state and excludes contamination between different S sectors. It does not, by itself, determine magnetic order or establish a quantum spin liquid: finite antiferromagnetic, valence-bond, and spin-liquid ground states may all be singlets. The intended advance is therefore a controlled and independently testable route to physical identification, rather than an answer guaranteed by the architecture.

The program has three linked deliverables. First, small-system exact benchmarks must validate not only energies, but also total spin, momentum and little-group labels, level hierarchies, and transition weights. Second, these validated quantum numbers must be retained as system size increases beyond exact diagonalization, while energy and observable quality are checked against independent methods and systematic width/training sequences. Third, the resulting spectra should determine whether the low-energy families at J2=0.125 remain continuously organized as a 120-degree antiferromagnetic rotor, or instead exhibit a distinct pattern compatible with a candidate spin-liquid theory and stable with increasing size.

Competitive energies are necessary to resolve the relevant branches, but beating every published ground-state energy is not the sole criterion. Comparisons must use the same torus, boundary conditions, Hamiltonian, and spatial sector, distinguish raw from Lanczos-improved states, and budget excitation errors in total-energy units. Exact spin labels cannot compensate for unresolved variational errors. High-quality ordinary neural states provide independent checks; DMRG and iPEPS remain complementary references.
